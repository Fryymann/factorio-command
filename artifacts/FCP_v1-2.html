How the math works: buildings = (items needed per second ÷ items per craft) × craft time ÷ building speed. In Buildings mode the target is that many buildings of the final product running flat out. Counts are rounded up; the small grey number is the exact value.

Not modelled yet: modules, beacons, productivity bonuses, oil refining (petroleum gas, heavy oil and water are treated as inputs), foundry, electromagnetic plant, and recipes from other planets.

Recipe data is typed in from the 2.0 recipes. Items tagged check are ones I'm less sure of; compare them against the in-game tooltip.

    Whole chain lays every module out on one main bus: raw inputs and each product get a bus column on the left, and each module becomes one or more rows of machines fed from the bus. Modules making the same item are merged into one column. A single module gets the same treatment on its own, with its ingredients as bus inputs, so you can build and test it by itself.

    Rows split when an input lane or the output belt would be over capacity, or past the max per row. Each row's input belt carries two ingredients on separate lanes. A second belt with long-handed inserters carries a third and fourth.

    Editing: pick a single module, then Edit layout to get an editable copy. Machines can be moved but not added or removed; belts, undergrounds, inserters and poles are yours. Belt lanes update as you edit. Splitters from the generated layout are kept and mix their inputs; placing new splitters and side-loading onto undergrounds or splitters aren't modelled yet. Inserters lined up with a belt are assumed to drop on its right lane.

    Before you build it: fluids are drawn as pipe guides and left out of the blueprint, so place pipes by hand. Burner furnaces get a coal lane for fuel that the Modules tab doesn't count. Inserter speeds are approximate. The tile layout generator comes from your friend's version of this planner.
